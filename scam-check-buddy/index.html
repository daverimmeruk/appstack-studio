📖 Scam Check Buddy Manual

Everything below is the same text as the Manual tab in the app.

What Scam Check Buddy does

Scam Check Buddy reads a message you're not sure about and lists the warning signs it finds, so you can decide before you tap a link, reply or pay. It never says a message is safe: no app can prove that. It only says what it found.

To keep this manual, tap 📤 Share the manual at the top of the Manual tab: it makes a PDF to save, email or print.

Check tab: checking a message

  • Paste: copy the message in your messages, email or WhatsApp app (long-press it, then Copy), then tap 📋 Paste.
  • Share: in many apps you can tap Share on a message and pick Scam Check Buddy.
  • Select: select the text in any app and pick Scam check from the menu that appears (on some phones under ⋮ or "More").
  • 📷 Scan a QR code: shows the web address in a QR code and checks it, without opening it. The scanner comes from Google Play services, so the app doesn't need the camera permission.
  • You can also type or paste a single web address.
  • The check runs on the phone straight away, with no internet.

What it looks for

In the web addresses:

  • Lookalikes: an address that uses a company's name but isn't its website, for example "royalmail-redelivery.top" (also with numbers for letters, like "r0yalmail").
  • A message that names a company (for example Evri, HMRC or PayPal) but links somewhere else.
  • Short links (bit.ly, tinyurl.com and others) that hide where they go.
  • Tricks: special letters that look like normal ones, a bare number instead of a name, or a real company name before an @ that hides the real website.
  • Endings that are cheap to register and rarely used by real companies (for example .top or .xyz). This is information, not proof.

In the message:

  • Rushing you, small fees, delivery problems, refunds, account or payment problems, "new number" family messages, prizes, investment returns and unpaid tolls or fines.
  • Asking you to move money to a "safe" account, to pay with gift cards, to share a code or PIN, or to allow remote access.
  • Asking you to call a number.

🌍 Any currency: fees and money requests are found in any currency, with a sign or a code, before or after the number (for example £1.99, €1,99, 1,99 €, $2.99, ₹25, Rs. 30, ¥300 or 1.99 USD), and phone numbers from any country.

Checking the links online

  • Tap 🌐 Check the links online after a check that found links.
  • The official domain register for each address is asked when the website was registered (RDAP, the public record that replaced WHOIS). Scam websites are usually days or weeks old: under 90 days is a strong warning sign, under a year a sign to be careful.
  • Short links: the short-link service is asked where its link points. The app reads the answer but doesn't follow it, so the website at the end is never opened and can't see that you checked it.
  • The address a short link leads to is then checked the same way as the others.
  • Only the web addresses are sent, never the rest of your message.
  • Free: 3 online checks a day. Pro: as many as you like.

Reading the result

  • 🔴 Strong warning signs: don't tap any link, reply or pay. Contact the company using its own app, its website typed in yourself, or the number on your card.
  • 🟡 Be careful: check with the company or person another way before you do anything.
  • No warning signs found: that doesn't prove the message is genuine. If it asks for money, details or a code, check with the sender another way first.
  • ℹ️ lines are information, for example a company's own website or how old a website is.
  • 🛡️ What to do opens the Tips tab.

Tips tab

What to do if a message might be a scam, the official ways to report it in your country, and the most common scams there with how to spot them.

🌍 Your country is found from your SIM card or the phone's settings and shown at the top of the Tips tab. Tap 🌍 Change country to pick another one. The UK, Ireland, the USA, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Germany, France, Spain, Italy, the Netherlands and India each have their own couriers, tax office and reporting numbers; for any other country the Tips tab gives advice that is true everywhere, without local numbers. The check itself works the same in every country.

Pro

Pro is one payment for life, with no subscription. It adds:

  • ⭐ Online checks as often as you like (Free: 3 a day)
  • ⭐ Every check saved on the Results tab, newest first; delete one or clear all
  • ⭐ Share a result as a PDF, for example to show a relative or your bank

Everything else is free. Tap GO PRO at the top to buy or to restore a purchase on a new phone.

Privacy

  • No account and no sign-in.
  • The messages you check stay on this phone. With Pro, saved checks are kept on this phone only; they aren't backed up to the cloud or copied to a new phone.
  • The online check sends only the web addresses: the domain name to its official register, and a short link to its short-link service.
  • Scam Check Buddy can't read your texts, emails or chats by itself: it only sees what you paste, share or select.
  • A PDF only leaves the phone if you share it.

🛡️ WHAT TO DO — FOR YOUR COUNTRY (Tips tab)

The Tips tab shows the advice and the official ways to report a scam for your country, found from your SIM card or the phone's settings (🌍 Change country to pick another). Full country versions: the UK, Ireland, the USA, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Germany, France, Spain, Italy, the Netherlands and India; every other country gets advice that is true everywhere, without local numbers.

For example, in the UK:

  • Don't tap the link, reply, call the number in it or pay.
  • Contact the company using its own app, its website typed in yourself, or the number on your card or bill.
  • A call or message that says it's your bank: hang up and call 159 to reach your bank safely.
  • Forward a scam text to 7726 (free on UK networks) to report it.
  • Forward a scam email to report@phishing.gov.uk to report it.
  • If you've lost money: call your bank straight away and report it to Report Fraud (reportfraud.police.uk or 0300 123 2040 in England, Wales and Northern Ireland) or to the police.
