RSRP, RSRQ and SINR Explained: What Your 4G/5G Signal Numbers Mean

Signal bars only tell part of the story. Here is what RSRP, RSRQ and SINR mean, which numbers are good, and how to use them to find the best spot for a 4G or 5G router.

Why bars aren't enough

Your phone's signal bars are a rough summary, and every maker draws them differently. Engineers look at three numbers instead: RSRP (how strong the signal is), RSRQ (how clean it is) and SINR (how far it stands out above the noise). Together they explain why one spot gives fast, steady 4G or 5G and another doesn't, even with the same number of bars.

RSRP: signal strength

RSRP (Reference Signal Received Power) is the power of the signal from the mast, in dBm. The numbers are negative: the closer to zero, the stronger. As a rule of thumb for 4G and 5G:

  • -80 dBm or better: excellent
  • -80 to -90 dBm: good
  • -90 to -100 dBm: fair, so try nearer a window
  • Below -100 dBm: poor

For fast home broadband from a 4G or 5G router, aim for better than about -85 dBm.

RSRQ: signal quality

RSRQ (Reference Signal Received Quality) compares the useful signal with everything else your phone receives on that channel, including other cells and busy traffic. It is in dB and also negative. Better than -10 dB is a good target. A strong RSRP with a poor RSRQ often means interference from other cells or a busy mast.

SINR: signal against noise

SINR (Signal to Interference plus Noise Ratio) shows how far the signal stands above the noise, in dB, and here higher is better. It has the biggest effect on speed: above about 15 dB is a good target, and a high SINR usually means faster downloads and uploads.

Use them to place your router

Walk slowly round your home with the app open: every reading becomes a coloured circle on the map, and a ⭐ strong spot marks the best signal so far. The 💡 Tower Window Guide then suggests putting your gateway at the window where the signal reads highest, about 1.2 m above the floor and away from dense concrete or foil insulation.

Two phones side by side can show different numbers from the same mast, because their modems, antennas and software differ. Compare readings taken on the same phone.
