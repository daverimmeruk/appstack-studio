Which Phone Mast Is My Phone Connected To? How to Find Out on Android

Your phone talks to one mobile mast at a time. Here is how to see which cell you are on, how far away the mast is and which way your signal gets stronger, from your own phone's readings.

Your phone uses one cell at a time

A phone mast usually carries several cells, each covering one direction on one frequency band. Your phone connects to one of them (the serving cell) and keeps listening to the others nearby, ready to switch if one becomes stronger. Knowing which cell you are on, and roughly where its mast is, helps you understand why the signal is good in one room and poor in another.

Step 1: See the cell you are connected to

Open Cell Tower Finder and tap 🗼 Cell Tower. Allow location when asked: Android requires precise location before any app can read cell details.

The card on the map shows your carrier, the Cell ID of the cell you are connected to and its band (the frequency it uses). The signal line shows the measured strength in dBm next to your phone's own signal bars, the same as the icon in your status bar.

Best Tower lists every cell your phone can hear right now, with its band and signal, so you can see whether a stronger cell is nearby.

Step 2: How far away is the mast?

While mobile data is in use, the network tells your phone how far away the mast is (a measurement called timing advance). Cell Tower Finder shows it as "Distance: ≈…m (network-measured)" and draws a dashed ring on the map: the mast is somewhere on that ring.

Step 3: Walk to find the direction

Your phone doesn't know which direction a mast is in; it only knows it is receiving a signal. So the app works it out from your own readings. Walk slowly round your home and garden, front and back. Every reading becomes a coloured circle on the map (🟢 strong, 🟡 fair, 🔴 weak), and a ⭐ strong spot marks the best signal measured so far.

Once you have readings from different sides of the house, the small arrow shows which way your signal gets stronger. That is usually the side facing the mast, but walls and reflections can mislead it, so treat it as a guide. With readings from far enough apart, for example with the app open on a walk down your road, the app locates the mast from your readings and draws the line to it.

GPS indoors is only accurate to about 5–15 m, so the map tells you which side or end of the house is best rather than which shelf.

Step 4: Read the numbers

For 4G and 5G, signal strength (RSRP) is measured in dBm: the closer to zero, the better.

  • -80 dBm or better: excellent
  • -80 to -90 dBm: good
  • -90 to -100 dBm: fair, so try nearer a window
  • Below -100 dBm: poor; an external antenna, a booster or Wi-Fi Calling may help

Two phones side by side can show different numbers from the same mast, because their modems, antennas and software differ. Compare readings on the same phone.

Test the mast's speed

Tap ⚡ Test Tower Speed for a real download and upload test (about 30 MB of data at most). Turn Wi-Fi off first, otherwise you are testing your broadband, not the mast.

Why not use a mast map website?

Crowd-sourced mast maps can be approximate or out of date, and they can't tell you which cell your own phone is using right now. Cell Tower Finder works only from what your phone measures, and your readings and mast positions are stored only on your phone.
