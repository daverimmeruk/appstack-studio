Weak Wi-Fi in One Room? How to Find the Best Room for Your Router

Wi-Fi fine in the lounge but useless in the back bedroom? Here is how to measure each room with your own phone, see where an extender would help and pick the best room for your router.

Why one room has weak Wi-Fi

Wi-Fi gets weaker with distance and with every wall, floor and appliance it passes through. Thick walls, metal (radiators, mirrors, fridges) and a router tucked in a cupboard or behind the TV all cut the signal before it reaches the far rooms. The fix is usually not a faster package but a better spot for the router, or an extender in the right place. To find either, you need a reading from each room, taken on the same phone.

Step 1: Check each room on the Coverage screen

Open CellCoverage 5g and tap 📶 Coverage. Walk to the room furthest from your router. The heading line shows your live Wi-Fi reading, for example "From Wi-Fi -59 dBm (68%)", and the screen changes colour with it:

  • Green (75% or more, about -55 dBm or better): strong signal, no extender needed. Right next to the router (90% or more) the screen flashes green.
  • Amber (50% or more, down to about -70 dBm): good reception; moving the router a little can help.
  • Red, down to -80 dBm: weak signal; a Wi-Fi extender or mesh node may help here.
  • Red, weaker than -80 dBm: a dead zone; an extender or mesh node is recommended.

If a room shows red, tap 📶 Extender Node Guide: an extender works best about halfway between the router and the weak room, not in the weak room itself.

Step 2: Compare rooms with the Blueprint (Pro)

Tap 📐 Blueprint and choose Room A, Room B or Room C at the top (long-press a tab to rename it or add rooms). Stand in that room and drag the yellow router icon (📡) to where you are standing: a real Wi-Fi reading is recorded for the room. Take at least 3 readings per room, because Wi-Fi varies from moment to moment.

Then tap 🏆 Compare All Rooms. The rooms are ranked by their middle (median) Wi-Fi reading and its coverage score, and rooms you haven't measured are listed as not measured. If the top rooms are within 2 dB of each other, they are effectively a tie. 📄 Export Floorplan Report shares the result as a PDF.

Step 3: Put the router where it helps most

Readings tell you where the signal is weak; a few habits get the most out of the router once you move it:

  • Raise it off the floor and keep it in the open, not inside a cupboard or behind the TV.
  • Keep it away from metal, mirrors and large appliances.
  • Somewhere central usually covers more rooms than a corner of the house.

Measure again after moving it: the same phone, the same rooms, so the numbers are comparable.

Is it the Wi-Fi or the mobile signal?

If your broadband comes from a 4G or 5G router, the mobile signal it receives matters too. CellCoverage 5g's 🗼 Step 1: Cell screen shows your live mobile connection, measured by the phone: network, 4G or 5G, band, signal level and your phone's own signal bars. Find the window where it reads highest and put the 4G/5G router there; then use the Coverage screen to check the Wi-Fi in the other rooms.
